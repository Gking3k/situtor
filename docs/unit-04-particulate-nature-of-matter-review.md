## Curriculum coverage

The lesson sequence is based on the Federal Ministry of Education Inspire SS1 Particulate Nature of Matter learning outcomes and Rina's handwritten Chemistry notes. The source materials cover atoms, molecules, ions, Dalton's theory, subatomic particles, electron arrangement, isotopes and relative atomic mass. Supplementary verified references are used to clarify model history and scientific definitions.

## Formula and equation teaching standard

Every formula and equation used in this unit must be explained in plain language before students are expected to apply it. The lessons and worked examples follow this sequence:

1. Identify the purpose of the formula or equation.
2. Define every letter, symbol, subscript, superscript, coefficient, sign, arrow and state symbol.
3. Explain what quantity or particle each part represents.
4. Substitute the known values one step at a time.
5. Show the arithmetic and retain units where the quantity has units.
6. Check the result and explain whether it is reasonable.

Examples include:

- Ion charge: net charge = number of protons − number of electrons.
- Mass number: A = Z + N; therefore neutron number N = A − Z.
- Relative atomic mass: weighted average of isotope masses using fractional or percentage abundances.
- Balanced reaction: `2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)`, with coefficients, subscripts, formula units and state symbols explained.

Never change a chemical formula's subscripts to balance an equation. Balance by changing coefficients.

## Diagram standard

Diagrams are original Si Tutor vector illustrations, not copied textbook images. Each diagram uses a title/accessible description and a caption explaining what it represents and any simplifications. The set includes:

- Particle-level comparison of a physical change and a chemical reaction.
- Historical atomic-model timeline.
- Rutherford alpha-particle scattering with straight-through, deflected and backscattered paths.
- Atom structure showing nucleus, protons, neutrons, electrons and shell guides.
- Electron arrangements for representative elements.
- Carbon-12 and carbon-14 particle-count comparison with a particle legend.
- Weighted-average isotope-abundance visual.
- s and p orbital probability-shape illustration and orbital-box occupancy.

Particle positions and distances are schematic, not scale models. Shell diagrams are introductory representations, not literal electron tracks. Orbital material is explicitly identified as enrichment.

## Assessment standard for this unit

Each of the nine lessons has a 12-question quiz (108 questions total). The upgraded questions use a mixture of:

- Application to unfamiliar but age-appropriate situations.
- Interpretation of diagrams, models, tables and reaction equations.
- Multi-step calculations for particle counts, charges, mass numbers and isotope averages.
- Error analysis and misconception correction.
- Comparison and scientific reasoning.
- Four plausible answer options, one correct response and an explanatory review after submission.

Calculation and equation questions are included where relevant to the lesson; orbital enrichment questions are conceptual and introductory rather than advanced quantum calculations. The shared quiz experience still withholds answer feedback until submission.

## Scientific/editorial decisions

- **Carbon-12 reference:** Relative atomic mass and the unified atomic mass unit are taught using the carbon-12 reference. The Ministry Inspire page has a learning-objective wording that refers to C-14 in this context; that wording conflicts with the IUPAC definition. Si Tutor uses the scientifically correct carbon-12 reference and flags the discrepancy here for educator awareness.
- **Chemical versus nuclear change:** Conservation of atoms and element identity statements refer to ordinary chemical reactions. Nuclear reactions can change a nucleus and may transform one element into another.
- **Shell notation:** The first-20-element shell pattern is taught at SS1 level. The lesson does not imply that the third shell can never hold more than eight electrons; it explains that the simplified first-20 pattern fills 2, 8, 8, 2.
- **Isotope notation:** Atomic number is the lower-left number and identifies proton count; mass number is the upper-left number and equals protons plus neutrons.
- **Orbital enrichment:** Shell, subshell and orbital vocabulary is useful extension material. It is not presented as a mandatory prerequisite for the main SS1 outcomes.
- **Molecule and formula-unit distinction:** Molecular substances and ionic lattices are not conflated. Solid sodium chloride is described in terms of formula units in a repeating ionic lattice, not discrete NaCl molecules.
