## Teaching design

Each lesson begins with a plain-language main idea, defines unfamiliar vocabulary before using it, develops each process in logical stages and ends with a recap. Chemical equations include meanings of formulae, coefficients and state symbols, plus atom-count or mole-ratio checks where relevant. Worked calculations show the given data, method, units and conclusion.

Eight SVG diagram instances are included across the six lessons (seven distinct designs, with the limewater test reused where it is taught again). They cover carbon allotropes, coalification/destructive distillation, carbon oxides, the carbon dioxide/limewater test, petroleum geological traps, fractional distillation, and cracking/reforming. Each has an SVG title and description, a caption and an independently scrollable viewport on narrow screens. The latest visual refinement pass separates all process labels from their connector arrows, uses line-wrapped labels where needed, and keeps annotations inside their intended panels.

## Scientific and editorial decisions

- Carbon has atomic number 6 and electron arrangement 2,4; tetravalency is explained as common covalent bonding, not formation of a simple C4+ ion.
- Diamond and graphite are treated as carbon allotropes. Graphite conduction is linked to delocalised electrons; softness is linked to sliding layers. Graphene is described as one layer; fullerene C60 is a 60-carbon cage.
- Peat is identified as the precursor to coal rather than one of the four main coal ranks. Rank sequence is given as lignite, sub-bituminous, bituminous and anthracite after peat.
- Coalification is kept separate from industrial destructive distillation. Coke is described as a carbon-rich residue, not pure carbon. Gasification is introduced with the hot-carbon/steam equation.
- Carbon monoxide is explicitly described as colourless, odourless and highly poisonous. Learners are told not to rely on smell and not to operate generators indoors. No unsafe production demonstration is suggested.
- Carbon monoxide is diagrammed with a triple bond (C≡O), while carbon dioxide is diagrammed as linear O=C=O with two double bonds. Carbon dioxide is distinguished from carbon monoxide; the limewater test and excess-CO₂ clearing reaction are explained.
- Carbonate and hydrogencarbonate ions have distinct formulae and charges: CO3^2- and HCO3^-. Acid reaction equations and thermal decomposition equations are balanced and explained.
- Crude oil is presented as a complex mixture stored in porous reservoir rock, not an underground lake. Geological trapping is shown as a simplified model.
- Fractional distillation is distinguished from chemical conversion. Fractions are described as mixtures with overlapping boiling ranges; exact outlets and ranges vary by refinery. Cracking breaks molecules; reforming rearranges them.
- The catalytic reforming example is explicitly labelled illustrative rather than a universal industrial equation. The diagram distinguishes a straight-chain alkane feed from a cyclic reforming product and notes the link to petrol octane rating.
- The petroleum-formation diagram now separates the three geological stages, labels the cap rock, overlying sedimentary rocks, source rock, porous reservoir, gas cap, oil, formation water and production well, and keeps the wellbore callouts clear.
- The limewater-test delivery-tube label sits in its own callout above the pipe instead of crossing the tube riser; the balanced limewater reaction is shown beneath the apparatus.
