# Unit 09 — Water: content and accuracy review

## Scope

The SS1 Chemistry Water topic is mapped to five lessons:

1. Sources and types of water
2. Properties and uses of water
3. Hardness and treatment of water
4. Water pollution and purity
5. Laboratory preparation of water

## Scientific distinctions emphasised

- Surface water is found above ground; groundwater is stored in soil and water-bearing rock layers. A protected source is not automatically safe.
- Freshwater is not the same classification as soft water. Pure water and potable water are also different concepts.
- Hardness is mainly associated with dissolved calcium and magnesium ions. Temporary hardness is commonly associated with hydrogencarbonates; permanent hardness commonly with sulfates and chlorides.
- Boiling can reduce temporary hardness but does not remove ordinary permanent hardness. Washing soda and ion exchange are included as other softening methods.
- Softening is not disinfection. Removing calcium and magnesium ions does not prove that microorganisms have been inactivated.
- Water-treatment stages are described according to their purpose: screening, coagulation/flocculation, sedimentation, filtration, disinfection and protected storage.
- Clear appearance, normal smell or pH alone cannot establish drinking-water safety. Microbial and chemical tests assess different risks.
- Distillation is a physical separation process; electrolysis decomposes water; hydrogen and oxygen synthesis forms water chemically. These processes are not presented as interchangeable.
- Hydrogen–oxygen ignition is identified as hazardous and is not given as an unsupervised student experiment.

## Equation checks

- Water formation: `2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)` — 4 H atoms and 2 O atoms on each side.
- Water electrolysis: `2H2O(l) -> 2H2(g) + O2(g)` — 4 H atoms and 2 O atoms on each side; gas-volume ratio H2:O2 is 2:1 at the same temperature and pressure.
- Temporary hardness example: `Ca(HCO3)2(aq) -> CaCO3(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(l)`.
- Permanent hardness example: `CaSO4(aq) + Na2CO3(aq) -> CaCO3(s) + Na2SO4(aq)`.
- Soap scum model: `2C17H35COONa(aq) + CaCl2(aq) -> (C17H35COO)2Ca(s) + 2NaCl(aq)`.
- Hydration test: `CuSO4(s) + 5H2O(l) -> CuSO4·5H2O(s)`.
