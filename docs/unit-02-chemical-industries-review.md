## Curriculum-derived scope

The Federal Ministry of Education Inspire SS1 Chemistry resource for Chemical Industries states that learners should:

- identify chemical industries in their locality;
- explain how these industries influence their lives and the national economy, especially in Nigeria;
- describe environmental problems created by chemical industries; and
- suggest solutions to those problems.

Source: https://inspire.education.gov.ng/lessons/234/preview

## Supplementary scope and editorial choices

The five-lesson structure follows the existing Si Tutor curriculum catalogue. Additional explanations on raw-material sources, by-products, industry categories, location factors, biotechnology and biogas are supplementary learning content, not presented as verbatim curriculum wording.

- The distinction between heavy/bulk and fine chemicals is introductory. Industry-specific definitions and boundaries may vary; the lesson explains the general scale and specialisation distinction and warns against interpreting “heavy” as physical weight.
- Nigerian examples are described by sector (cement, petrochemicals/plastics, fertiliser, pharmaceuticals and food processing). The lessons do not claim that every input is locally sourced or that a named company makes a particular product.
- A by-product may be useful but is not automatically harmless. It requires assessment and safe handling. Waste that is not recovered must be managed appropriately.
- Lesson 1 uses a direct, step-by-step explanation of how industries select raw materials. No humorous analogy is used.
- Biogas is described as a gas mixture, mainly methane and carbon dioxide, not as pure methane. Digestate may be useful only when appropriately assessed and managed.
- The biogas lesson includes safety information: methane is combustible and hydrogen sulphide can be harmful. No unsupervised student digester or ignition activity is recommended.
- Environmental controls distinguish pollution prevention at source from downstream treatment. The preferred hierarchy begins with preventing pollution where feasible.
- The local-industry activity is based on safe observation and public information. Students must not enter restricted factory areas or touch, taste or deliberately smell unknown substances.

1. Confirm Unit 02 coverage against the scheme used by the target school.
2. Check examples of industrial raw materials and products for accuracy and SS1 suitability.
3. Confirm the level of detail for heavy/bulk and fine chemicals.
4. Review all biotechnology and biogas descriptions, including safety wording.
5. Review the environmental issues and proposed controls.
6. Check every quiz question, distractor, answer and explanation.
7. Confirm that Lesson 1 explains raw-material selection clearly and that the examples are accurate and appropriate for SS1.
