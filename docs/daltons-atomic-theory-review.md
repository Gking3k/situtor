## Editorial and scientific decisions

1. The lesson uses five classroom-friendly statements that synthesize the common Dalton postulates. Textbooks may group and number the postulates differently; the page says this explicitly.
2. Dalton's indivisible solid-sphere model is labelled as a historical model, not a current depiction of atomic structure.
3. The conservation postulate is explicitly limited to ordinary chemical reactions. The lesson distinguishes this from nuclear reactions, which change nuclei and can change an element's identity.
4. Isotopes are described as atoms with the same number of protons (same atomic number) but different numbers of neutrons and therefore different mass numbers.
5. Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are used as the isotope example: both have six protons; carbon-12 has six neutrons and carbon-14 has eight.
6. The balanced equation `2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O` is used to illustrate conservation and rearrangement of atoms during a chemical reaction.
7. `CO` and `CO₂` are used to illustrate that the same two elements can form more than one compound in different simple whole-number ratios.
8. The lesson text, diagram and quiz explanations are original Si Tutor material. External resources are cited as further reading; no external diagrams or long passages are reproduced.

## Quiz coverage

Ten multiple-choice items assess: Dalton's contribution; the atom concept; similarity of atoms of an element; simple whole-number ratios; rearrangement in chemical reactions; the electron discovery; isotopes; nuclear transformations; CO/CO₂ ratios; and a modern interpretation of Dalton's theory.
