# Si Tutor — Lesson Authoring Guidelines

These guidelines apply to every subject and unit.

## Teaching tone

- Use a clear, respectful and age-appropriate tone.
- Do not use funny analogies, joke-based examples or comedy as a teaching device.
- Keep lessons engaging through explanation quality, carefully chosen examples, diagrams, activities and questions.

## Explain complex ideas in steps

1. Introduce the idea with a plain-language definition.
2. Define unfamiliar scientific terms before relying on them.
3. Divide multi-stage processes into an ordered sequence.
4. Explain the purpose of each stage and how it connects to the next.
5. Use accurate examples drawn from Chemistry and familiar real-world contexts. Do not force a comparison if it could create a misconception.
6. For every formula or equation, explain each symbol, subscript, coefficient, sign/arrow and state symbol; identify what each quantity means; substitute values one step at a time; track units; and explain why the final answer makes sense.
7. Use diagrams, tables or labelled process flows when they make relationships easier to see.
8. End substantial sections with a short recap or key-point note.

## Accuracy and source handling

- Distinguish curriculum-derived requirements from supplementary explanations and examples.
- Prefer authoritative educational, scientific and standards sources for researched additions.
- Do not copy textbook prose or illustrations.
- Include safety limits for practical activities and avoid instructions that expose learners to hazardous substances or equipment.
- Review quiz questions, distractors, answer keys and explanations against the lesson content.

## Assessment challenge and question design

- Use questions that test understanding and transfer, not only recall of definitions.
- Include application, data/diagram interpretation, comparison, reasoning and error-analysis questions where appropriate.
- Use more demanding but fair distractors based on realistic misconceptions; do not rely on trick wording.
- Use at least 12 questions per lesson as the default assessment target unless the subject or lesson structure makes a different count more appropriate.
- Include multi-step calculations in lessons where quantitative reasoning is part of the learning objective. Show a full worked solution in the answer explanation.
- Include chemical equations where relevant. Ask students to interpret formulae, coefficients, subscripts, state symbols, conservation of atoms, or balancing where those ideas have been taught.
- Do not force calculations or equations into lessons where they do not naturally belong.
- Explanations should teach why the correct option is right and, where useful, why a tempting alternative is wrong.
- Before release, audit existing quizzes for difficulty and consistency; upgrades to new units do not automatically update earlier unit quizzes.

## Learner level

Write for the specified class level. Introduce technical depth gradually and explain necessary vocabulary; do not oversimplify a scientific definition to make it sound entertaining.
