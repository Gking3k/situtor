# Unit 05 — Symbols, Formulae and Equations

## Scope and curriculum basis

The unit follows the Federal Ministry of Education Inspire SS1 lesson overview for Symbols, Formulae and Equations. Its stated outcomes include symbols of the first 20 elements and common elements, element valencies, empirical and molecular formulae, conservation of matter, constant composition, multiple proportions and chemical equations.

The five lesson sequence is:

1. Chemical symbols and valency
2. Naming compounds
3. Empirical and molecular formulae
4. Laws of chemical combination
5. Writing and balancing chemical equations

Supplementary detail on relative formula mass, molar mass and percentage composition is included in Lesson 3 because these calculations are closely connected to formula determination and appear in commonly used Nigerian SS1 lesson-note schemes. The law of reciprocal proportions is mentioned briefly as an extension only; it is not represented as one of the three core laws in the FME Inspire Unit 5 overview.

## Teaching and accuracy decisions

- Symbols use the standard capital/lowercase convention (for example, Co is cobalt; CO is a formula containing carbon and oxygen).
- Valency is taught as combining capacity. For simple ions its magnitude corresponds to the ion charge, but valency and oxidation number are not treated as interchangeable in every context.
- Ionic formulae are derived from charge neutrality. The cross-over shortcut is always checked by calculating the total positive and negative charges. Polyatomic ions are kept intact, with parentheses when more than one is required.
- Ionic compounds are named cation first, then anion. Monatomic anions commonly take the -ide ending; polyatomic ions retain their established names. Roman numerals identify a variable-charge metal's oxidation state. Simple binary molecular compounds use numerical prefixes.
- Empirical formula calculations convert masses to moles before finding the simplest whole-number ratio. Molecular formula calculations use the ratio of molecular/relative mass to empirical formula mass. Relative molecular mass is dimensionless; molar mass has units such as g mol-1.
- Conservation of mass is discussed for a closed system. A gas escaping an open vessel explains an apparent loss from the measured vessel, not destruction of matter.
- Constant composition compares samples of the same pure compound. Multiple proportions holds the mass of one element fixed while comparing masses of the other element in different compounds.
- Equations are balanced by changing coefficients only. Subscripts are never changed to force a balance, because this changes the identity of the substance.
- State symbols are defined as (s), (l), (g) and (aq).

## Diagrams

The unit includes original, labelled vector diagrams for:

- First twenty element symbols and atomic numbers.
- Ionic charge balance, including simple ions and polyatomic ions.
- A decision guide for naming ionic and molecular compounds.
- The empirical-to-molecular formula calculation sequence.
- The three core laws of chemical combination.
- Particle-level atom accounting in the balanced formation of water.

All diagrams are schematic. Their captions explain what is represented and identify the limits of simplification.

## Assessment

Each lesson has 12 multiple-choice questions (60 questions total). Questions include formula writing, nomenclature, multi-step empirical/molecular formula calculations, relative mass and percentage composition, mass-ratio reasoning, state symbols, atom counting and balancing equations. Answer explanations show the relevant calculation or reasoning after submission.
