# Unit 07 — Gas Laws: Content and review notes

## Coverage
The seven lesson IDs follow the existing Si Tutor catalogue. They cover the Federal Ministry of Education SS1 Gas Laws outcomes: Boyle’s law, Charles’ law, general gas equation, Gay-Lussac’s law, Avogadro’s law, ideal gas equation, Graham’s law, molar volume, Avogadro number/mole concept and calculations.

## Scientific distinctions made explicit

### Temperature
All proportional gas-law equations use absolute temperature in kelvin. The conversion used is `T(K) = θ(°C) + 273.15`; school calculations may round 273.15 to 273 when the question uses whole-number data.

### Boyle and Charles
- Boyle: `P₁V₁ = P₂V₂`, with fixed amount and constant temperature.
- Charles: `V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂`, with fixed amount and constant pressure.

### Combined and pressure–temperature laws
- Combined: `P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂` for the same fixed amount of gas.
- At fixed volume and amount: `P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂`. It is called Amontons’ law or the pressure law; some school material calls it Gay-Lussac’s pressure law.

### Gay-Lussac’s law of combining volumes
Balanced equation coefficients give simple whole-number ratios of reacting gas volumes only when volumes are measured at the same temperature and pressure. This is kept distinct from the pressure–temperature law.

### Dalton and Avogadro
- Dalton: total pressure is the sum of partial pressures of non-reacting gases in the mixture.
- Avogadro: at fixed temperature and pressure, gas volume is directly proportional to amount in moles.

### Mole and molar volume
The Avogadro constant is exactly `6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹`. The lesson labels `22.4 dm³ mol⁻¹` as the familiar school STP convention at `273.15 K` and `1 atm`. At `273.15 K` and `1 bar`, the ideal molar volume is about `22.71 dm³ mol⁻¹`; these conventions are not mixed.

### Ideal gas and Graham
- `PV = nRT`, with the selected R value matching pressure and volume units.
- Graham: `r₁/r₂ = √(M₂/M₁)`. The molar masses are reversed inside the square root because rate is inversely proportional to the square root of molar mass.

## Assessment design
Each lesson has 12 multi-choice questions. Items include direct application, graph or condition interpretation, unit conversion, multi-step arithmetic, equation-volume ratios, partial-pressure calculations, ideal-gas calculations, Graham rate ratios and misconception analysis. Correct options are distributed evenly: three each at A, B, C and D per quiz.

## Visual audit notes
- Boyle’s law: the before/after particle containers show the same ten particles; only the volume changes. A separate inverse P–V graph is included.
- Charles’ law: the warmer constant-pressure gas syringe is physically wider to represent larger volume; particle count is unchanged.
- Combined law: initial and final states have the same amount of gas and the formula is displayed separately.
- Dalton: container particle colours are keyed to nitrogen, oxygen and other gases; the pressure numbers add to the stated total.
- Molar volume: dots are symbolic, not a literal rendering of Avogadro-scale particle counts; conditions are printed explicitly.
- Ideal gas/Graham: the four ideal-gas variables are arranged around a particle container without text over the particles; a separate lower panel shows the diffusion tube and labels the meeting point. Graham’s law is sourced to OpenStax section 9.4.
