## Coverage in this lesson

1. Meaning of chemistry: composition, structure, properties and changes of matter.
2. Meaning of matter: anything that has mass and occupies space.
3. Three complementary ways chemists work: observable properties, particle-level explanations and symbolic representations.
4. Five traditional major branches: organic, inorganic, analytical, physical chemistry and biochemistry.
5. Importance of chemistry in health, agriculture/food, water/sanitation, energy, manufacturing/construction and environmental protection.
6. Everyday examples, including Nigerian contexts.
7. A ten-question quiz with explanations and a separate answer key.

## Editorial decisions and boundaries

- This lesson introduces meaning, branches and broad importance. The next Unit 01 lesson is reserved for specific careers and applications so career pathways can be treated properly rather than crowded into this overview.
- The following Unit 01 lessons cover chemical hazards/safety/environmental effects and the scientific method, matching the Federal Ministry learning outcomes.
- The five branches are described as traditional major divisions. They overlap, and the descriptions are introductory rather than exhaustive.
- Organic chemistry is described as the study of most carbon-containing compounds; the wording avoids the inaccurate claim that every compound containing carbon is classified as organic.
- The lesson does not imply that every chemical is dangerous or that chemistry alone solves complex health, agricultural or environmental problems.
- Examples such as water testing, soil analysis and battery chemistry are explanatory contexts, not instructions for unsupervised laboratory work.
- All lesson text, quiz questions and the hero illustration are original Si Tutor material. External resources are linked as further reading; no external diagrams or long passages are reproduced.

## Quiz coverage

Ten items check the meaning of chemistry, the meaning of matter, the five branches, their main questions, and examples of chemistry's contribution to agriculture and public health.
