# Unit 06 — Chemical Combination

## Status

All six lessons and their six quizzes are prepared for learner/educator review. Every completed lesson is available in development mode; payment entitlement is not enforced.

## Lesson sequence

1. **The periodic table** — atomic number, increasing atomic-number order, groups, periods and classification of the first twenty elements.
2. **Electronic configuration** — shell arrangements for the first twenty elements, outer-shell electrons, period/group connections and introductory ion arrangements.
3. **Ionic and covalent bonding** — transfer and sharing of electrons, ionic lattices, simple molecules, properties and limitations of broad property statements.
4. **Coordinate covalent and metallic bonding** — lone-pair donation, ammonium/hydronium examples, positive metal-ion cores, delocalised electrons and metal properties.
5. **Intermolecular forces** — intramolecular versus intermolecular, London dispersion, dipole–dipole attractions, hydrogen bonding and effects on physical properties.
6. **States of matter and kinetic theory** — particle arrangement and motion, compressibility, diffusion, gas pressure, phase changes and heating curves.

## Scope decisions

- The Federal Ministry of Education's SS1 Chemical Combination resource specifies the first twenty elements, electronic configurations, atomic-number-based periodic arrangement, bonding types, compound naming, states of matter and kinetic theory. The first-twenty-element scope is used for the core lessons.
- Compound naming has its own lesson in Unit 05. Unit 06 reinforces formula interpretation in bonding examples and directs learners back to Unit 05 for the complete naming systems rather than duplicating that lesson.
- The first-twenty shell model uses the familiar 2,8,8,2 sequence. The lesson explicitly warns that this is an introductory shell arrangement for the first twenty elements, not a universal maximum-capacity rule for every shell or a full orbital/subshell treatment.
- Coordinate covalent bonding is explained using NH3 + H+ → NH4+ and H2O + H+ → H3O+. The donor-arrow indicates where the electron pair originates during formation; the resulting four N—H bonds in NH4+ are described as equivalent.
- Metallic bonding uses the positive metal-ion-core/delocalised-electron model. The text avoids implying every metal is solid at room temperature or has an identical melting point.
- Hydrogen bonding is restricted to the usual introductory cases involving hydrogen directly bonded to N, O or F and attraction to a lone pair on N, O or F. The lesson distinguishes this intermolecular attraction from covalent bonds inside molecules.
- “Van der Waals forces” is flagged as a term used with different breadth in different textbooks; the lesson names London dispersion, dipole–dipole and hydrogen bonding separately.
- States and kinetic theory content remains qualitative. Numerical gas-law calculations are reserved for Unit 07.

## Assessment

- Six quizzes, twelve questions each (72 total).
- Questions test interpretation, application, comparison, error analysis and multi-step reasoning.
- Quantitative checks include atomic-number/electron totals, ion electron counts and charge balance.
- Equations are explained by reading each formula, coefficient, subscript, charge and arrow; calculations are broken into steps.
- No question depends on an unexplained advanced orbital model or on gas-law calculations from Unit 07.
